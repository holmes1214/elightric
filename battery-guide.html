Five minutes · no tools · reversible

Switching your battery to backup-only

If you're on NEM 2.0, your battery earns nothing from its daily cycle but still wears out doing it. Here's how to stop that in each of the three main apps — and how to tell whether it applies to you at all.

⚠ Check this first — it's the wrong move for most people

Do this only if both are true:

  • You're on NEM 2.0 or NEM 1.0 — in practice, your system was approved before April 15, 2023
  • You have a battery

On NEM 3.0 / NBT (approved April 2023 or later) this would cost you money — there the evening price spread is real and your battery is earning it. Don't do this.

Why it's free money on NEM 2.0

Under full net metering, every kilowatt-hour you export is credited at the full retail rate. So storing a kilowatt-hour to use at 8 p.m. and pushing it to the grid at noon are worth exactly the same on your bill. The battery captures no price spread.

But it still cycles — and every cycle uses up a slice of a warranty measured in throughput. You're spending battery life to buy nothing. Stopping the daily cycle is worth roughly $150–$600 a year in deferred wear for a typical 10–13.5 kWh battery, and costs you nothing on the bill.

Your backup capacity is unaffected. In fact it goes up: the battery sits full instead of half-drained.

Tesla Powerwall

Backup Reserve → 100%
  1. 1Open the Tesla app and select your energy site
  2. 2From the energy home screen, tap Settings
  3. 3Tap Powerwall
  4. 4Drag the Backup Reserve slider all the way to 100%

Tesla's own documentation confirms the effect: a backup reserve of 100% “prevents use of Self-Powered mode or Savings mode.” That is precisely what you want here — no daily cycling.

You'll see a warning that this “will negatively impact the potential economic benefits.” That warning is written for NEM 3.0 customers, where it's true. On NEM 2.0 there are no economic benefits to lose. Proceed.

There is no longer a separate “Backup-only” mode in the current app — the 100% reserve setting replaces it.

Source: Tesla Support — Backup Reserve

Enphase IQ Battery

Profile → Full Backup
  1. 1Open the Enphase app
  2. 2Tap Menu, then Settings
  3. 3Tap Battery
  4. 4Change the profile from Self-Consumption (or Savings) to Full Backup

Full Backup reserves the whole battery for outages and stops the daily charge-discharge cycle.

Depending on your app version the profile may be labelled Full backup or shown as a reserve slider you push to 100%. Either reaches the same place. If you only see Backup — partial, set the reserve to its maximum.

Source: Enphase — Know your storage system

SolarEdge Home Battery

Battery Mode → Backup Only
  1. 1Open the mySolarEdge app
  2. 2Tap the Battery icon in the bottom bar
  3. 3Go to Battery Mode → Battery Mode Panel
  4. 4Select Backup Only

SolarEdge describes Backup Only as keeping the battery fully charged and reserved for outages.

Two conditions from SolarEdge's own note: you must be listed as Site Owner on the account, and Backup Only appears only on backup-configured sites. If you don't see it, your system was likely installed without a backup interface — in that case there's nothing to switch, and the finding doesn't apply to you.

Source: SolarEdge — Set Battery Mode Control in mySolarEdge (June 2025)

After you switch

  • Check it held. Look again the next morning. Some apps quietly revert the setting after a firmware update.
  • Your bill shouldn't change. That's the point — you're saving battery life, not kilowatt-hours. If your bill goes up noticeably over the next two cycles, something else is going on and it's worth a look.
  • It's fully reversible. Same path, put it back.
  • Revisit it if your tariff changes. Adding capacity or moving to a new home can put you on NEM 3.0, and then the logic flips.

Menu paths verified against each manufacturer's current published documentation, linked above. Apps change — if a screen doesn't match, the vendor page is the authority, not this one.

The dollar figure is an estimate from an 8,760-hour energy model using published 2026 PG&E rates, not a promise. It reflects deferred battery wear, not a change in your bill.

This is general guidance about a setting you control. It isn't advice about your specific installation, and it doesn't change anything in your warranty or your contract.
